## Overview

MioSalon's landing page had become a standard SaaS website. This redesign turned it into a conversion-focused page that helps salon and spa owners find the right management software for their business.

  items={[

**Primary users:** owners and managers of salons, spas, med spas and aesthetic clinics.

**My role:** I led design, user testing and development from end to end. I worked with two teammates during early ideation, and with the product manager and the CEO throughout.

## The problem

> The original landing page suffered from low engagement and poor mobile usability, and failed to communicate its value to salon owners. A generic structure and weak visual storytelling meant missed conversions and high bounce rates.

Five problems stood out:

- Standard section layouts that didn't hold attention
- Weak visual storytelling that failed to convey value
- Significant mobile usability issues
- Slow load times
- A generic structure with no focus on the salon industry

### Target audience

Salon owners and managers aged 25 to 45. 78% of them use business tools on a mobile device. They are:

- Short on time
- Often managing several locations
- Guided by peer recommendations
- Price-sensitive, but willing to pay for efficiency

### Their pain points

- Managing appointments across several devices
- Little time to learn complex software
- Needing quick access to daily business numbers
- Worry about staff adoption and training
- Integration with existing payment systems

### The competition

The key competitors are Fresha, Zenoti, Mangomint, Salonist and Mindbody. Most of them:

- Design for desktop first
- Offer limited mobile optimisation for core features
- Have complex pricing structures
- Have lengthy onboarding
- Pay little attention to local markets

### How visitors behaved

- 65% of visits came from mobile devices

- Feature sections that visualised the product got strong engagement
- Visitors scrolled quickly past plain feature lists
- Ratings, awards, client logos and testimonials built credibility and reduced hesitation

## Strategy

The research pointed to three tools for the redesign.

**User journey map.** The emotional journey from discovery to conversion, with four key touchpoints: Discover, Landing, Explore and Convert.

**Conversion funnel.** A three-stage funnel with clear metrics at each stage.

**Exit-intent triggers.** On desktop: mouse leave, tab switches and browser actions, scroll behaviour, inactivity and form abandonment. On mobile: navigation actions, scroll patterns, inactivity, form abandonment and exit patterns.

## Design process

We started with a design sprint with the product manager and the CEO. Working remotely in Figma, we collected landing pages from forward-thinking brands and shared what we liked about each. We ended the sprint by sketching a quick solution together. Our schedules were packed, so the whole sprint took two hours.

In a separate session we agreed four design principles for the page. All four centre on our customers, so that the page attracts quality leads.

Then we sketched widely. The aim at this stage was to diverge first and converge later.

In the early wireframes we mapped the purpose of each section against our design principles. The primary goal was to reduce bounce rate and increase demo bookings.

We then turned these ideas into high-fidelity designs, experimenting with colours, shapes and layouts. The first pass felt all over the place, so we kept refining.

After several iterations we had a design solid enough to put in front of users. It was not the final design, and it didn't need to be. We fail fast in order to learn faster.

## Testing with 50 owners

Testing had to validate the mobile-first approach on four counts: clarity and first impressions, navigation and findability, mobile usability, and the lead-conversion elements.

### First-click testing

50 salon and spa owners completed four tasks: book a demo, find feature details, contact support and start a free trial. I measured task success, time to complete, navigation patterns and points of confusion.

I also collected survey feedback from 50 participants across hair salons (45%), spas (25%), nail salons (15%) and beauty clinics (15%).

Their concerns were filling in forms on mobile, the clarity of feature explanations and navigation on smaller screens.

### What changed after testing

- A persistent chat button, so support is always visible
- A redesigned pricing section that works on mobile
- Visual aids alongside feature explanations
- Form fields optimised for mobile input
- A clear section on the benefits of the mobile app

## Build

I built the responsive page myself in HTML, CSS and JavaScript. I worked mobile-first and focused on performance, interactive elements and cross-browser compatibility.

Those choices contributed directly to the result: a fast, responsive page that serves mobile and desktop visitors equally well.

## What I learned

Designing mobile-first for a B2B SaaS product in the beauty industry taught me four things:

- Business owners respond to visual storytelling
- Performance optimisation affects conversion
- Where you place a call to action matters
- Exit-intent strategies work on mobile too

To keep the page effective we monitor it continuously, keep the content fresh and stay on top of essential technical maintenance, so it keeps serving both visitors and the business.
